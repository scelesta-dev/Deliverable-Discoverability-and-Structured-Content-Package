Tips & Tutorials

It's not just about knowing your camera settings and taking good looking pictures. A photographer working in the field has to be ready to deal with all sorts of things: changing light, strange locations, the weather (which is always a surprise), and clients with all sorts of different expectations. Before you go into a session you need to talk to the client and establish what they're after in terms of the outcome, what sort of style they want, what their schedule is and what they expect from the finished images. It's all about communication, turning up on time and being professional in order to build a good relationship and create a good experience for both the client and yourself.

Preparation is absolutely vital to a successful shoot. You need to check your gear, charge your batteries, have your memory cards ready, clean your lenses and select the right kit for the job. It's always worth carrying some backup gear just in case. You should do your bit of research on the location beforehand and consider the lighting, the backgrounds, the environment etc. All of that will go a long way to helping you make the right creative decisions in the field.

Maintaining an orderly photography studio/workspace is just as important for productivity and efficiency. Your gear (camera, lenses, computer, external hard drives, editing kit etc) needs to be stored securely and be readily accessible. A comfortable working space with good lighting and a tidy desk will be of great assistance when you're working for long periods on an edit. Having a consistent file naming convention, photos in appropriate folders and regular backups will go a long way to preventing data loss and making it easy to find and track client projects.

Post production is another major stage in the photography workflow. You go through the session, you then review the images, you select them, you edit them and you export them before you hand them over to the client. Having a consistent post production workflow is key to maintaining image quality and also to being efficient. You have to be mindful of client privacy at all times, you need to be realistic about the times you're going to deliver images and you need to be very clear about what the images can and can't be used for and what the rights are. It's all about putting your technical knowledge, your creative problem solving, your organisation and your communication skills together to build a really good relationship with your client and to build a photography business that is sustainable and enjoyable in the long term.
