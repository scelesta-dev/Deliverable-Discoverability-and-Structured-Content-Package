Camera 101

Get familiar with the essential parts of a camera, including the lens, image sensor, viewfinder, and camera controls. Understanding camera anatomy is the first step toward becoming a confident photographer.

Camera anatomy

A camera is made up of a number of key components. You've got the camera body which contains all the internal bits and pieces, the lens which gathers the light and focuses it into the camera, and within the lens itself is the aperture which controls how much light gets through and has a direct bearing on the depth of field.

The shutter controls the length of time that light is allowed to fall on the sensor (or film in the case of a film camera) and the sensor itself is what converts the light that falls on it into the electronic information which is then used to create the digital photograph. Film cameras of course use light sensitive film rather than a sensor. The viewfinder is what you use to frame and compose your image. It may be an optical viewfinder or it may be an electronic viewfinder (depending on the camera).

External controls (shutter release button, mode dial, control buttons, LCD screen, etc.) let you adjust settings, view your photos, and navigate the camera's functions. While camera design varies from DSLR to mirrorless to compact, many of these external components serve the same functions. Knowing how these different elements work together is key to getting the most from your camera, making the right creative decisions, and ultimately getting the shot you want.
